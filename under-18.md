POCSO is short for the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act. It covers sexual assault, sexual harassment, and using a child in sexual images or videos. It applies to boys, girls and every child.

This page is honest about what will happen, because knowing in advance makes it less frightening.

## Adults must report

**If an adult knows that a child has been sexually abused, the law says they must tell the police.** This applies to parents, teachers, counsellors, doctors and everyone else. An adult who stays silent is breaking the law.
{: .key}

What this means for you:

- Once you tell an adult, it will be reported. Whether to report is not a choice the law gives to a child. The rule exists to protect you
- **You will not get into trouble** for telling someone or for reporting
- The police must inform the Child Welfare Committee, whose job is to make sure you are safe and cared for
- **If the person who hurt you is at home,** tell your school counsellor or call {% include call.html line="child" %}. The Child Welfare Committee makes sure someone else looks after you

## Your statement, at home

- A police officer comes to **your home or a place you choose**. You don't have to go to a police station
- The officer is usually a woman, and **should not be in uniform**
- A parent or someone you trust can be with you
- You must **never be kept at a police station overnight**
- A Magistrate may record your statement too, in private, with someone you trust present

## The doctor

- If you are a girl, a **woman doctor** examines you
- A parent or someone you trust is in the room
- It can happen **before any police report** is made, and it is free
- The doctor should explain each step. You can ask questions, take a break or say stop

## Special courts

- Your case goes to a **Special Court** set up for children
- **You will not have to see the person who hurt you.** The court uses a screen or a video link
- The hearing is **closed to the public**
- You can take breaks, and you should not be called back again and again
- The law says your evidence should be recorded within 30 days and the trial should finish within a year. **In practice it often takes longer.** Plan for that, and lean on your support person

## Throughout

- **Your name is protected.** Nobody may publish your name, photo, school, address or anything else that identifies you
- **You can have a support person** to help you through the whole process
- **You have the right to a lawyer,** and it can be free
- **You may be entitled to compensation,** and some of it can be paid early, before the case ends
