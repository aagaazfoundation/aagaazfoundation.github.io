**It is not your fault.** Not what you wore, not where you were, not whether you had been drinking, not whether you knew them, not whether you fought back.
{: .fault}

<div class="steps" markdown="1">

## Get safe

Go somewhere you feel safe. If you are in danger, call {% include call.html line="emergency" %}.

## Tell one person you trust

A parent, a counsellor, a teacher, a friend's parent. If you are under 18 you can call the Child Helpline on {% include call.html line="child" %}. Women can call {% include call.html line="women" %}, or the National Commission for Women on {% include call.html line="ncw" %} (for women 18 and older).

<div class="for-u18" data-tag="Under 18" markdown="1">

**Know this before you tell an adult.** If you tell an adult that someone has sexually abused you, the law says they must tell the police. This is true for teachers, counsellors, doctors and everyone else. The rule exists to protect you.

You can ask them: "What will happen next?" "Who will you tell?" "Can someone I trust be with me?"

</div>

<div class="for-adult" data-tag="18 or older" markdown="1">

**You decide what happens next.** Medical care, a police report and a trial are separate decisions. You can do one without the others, and you can take your time.

</div>

## If it was physical, protect the evidence if you can

Before a doctor sees you, if you can:

- Don't bathe, shower or wash
- Don't change your clothes. If you already have, put them in a paper bag, not plastic, and don't wash them
- Don't clean up the place where it happened

**It is natural to want to wash. If you already have, go anyway.** Your account, any injuries and other evidence still matter.
{: .note}

## Keep anything that shows what happened

Messages, screenshots, call logs, photos, and the names of anyone who saw or heard something. Don't delete anything, even if it is upsetting to look at.

## See a doctor. It is free

After rape, or sexual assault of anyone under 18, every hospital, government or private, must treat you free of cost. For the medical examination that can be used as evidence, **a government hospital is best**. The law says a government doctor should do it where one is available, and that is what the police and courts rely on.

- You can ask for a woman doctor, and someone you trust can be in the room
- The doctor should explain each step. You can ask questions, take a break or say stop
- **Two medicines only work if you start within 72 hours:** medicine to prevent HIV, and emergency contraception (some types work up to 120 hours). Ask for them

<div class="for-u18" data-tag="Under 18" markdown="1">

A doctor can examine and treat you before any police report has been made. The doctor will inform the police.

</div>

<div class="for-adult" data-tag="18 or older" markdown="1">

You don't need a police report first. The hospital has to inform the police, but you still choose whether to make a complaint or give a statement.

</div>

## If you give a statement

- Take your time. You can ask for breaks
- Say everything you remember, even small things or things that feel embarrassing. A full first account helps later
- It is fine to say "I don't know" or "I don't remember". Don't guess
- Ask for it to be read back to you before anything is signed, and correct anything that is wrong

<div class="for-u18" data-tag="Under 18" markdown="1">

You don't have to go to a police station. An officer comes to your home or a place you choose. The officer should be a woman and should not be in uniform. A parent or someone you trust can be with you.

</div>

<div class="for-adult" data-tag="18 or older" markdown="1">

You can report at any police station, wherever it happened. Your statement can be recorded at your home, by a woman officer.

</div>
