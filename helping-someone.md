If someone has chosen to tell you, they trust you. How you react in the first minute matters more than knowing the law.

## What to say

- "I believe you."
- "It's not your fault."
- "Thank you for telling me."
- "What do you need right now?"
- "I'll stay with you while we work out what to do."
{: .say}

## What not to say or do

- "Why didn't you say no?" or "Why didn't you tell me sooner?"
- "Are you sure?" or "Maybe they didn't mean it."
- "What were you wearing?" "Why were you there?" "Had you been drinking?"
- Don't push for details. Let them tell you as much or as little as they want
- Don't promise to keep it secret if they are under 18. You may not be able to
- Don't confront the person who did it. It can put them at risk and harm a case
- Don't post about it or tell other people
{: .dont}

## Then help with the next step

- If they are in danger, call {% include call.html line="emergency" %}
- If it happened in the last few days, time matters for medical care and evidence. Go through [What to do first](/first-steps/) together
- Offer to go with them: to a counsellor, a doctor or the police

## If they are under 18

**If you are an adult, you must report it.** The law says anyone who knows that a child has been sexually abused has to tell the police. Not reporting is an offence. This applies to parents, teachers, counsellors, coaches and relatives.
{: .key}

- You can report to the police or call the Child Helpline on {% include call.html line="child" %}
- **Don't investigate first.** You don't need proof, and you should not question the child for details. Listen, write down what they said in their own words, and report
- Tell the child honestly what you are going to do and why, before you do it
- **In a school,** follow the school's procedure and involve the counsellor straight away
- **If you are under 18 yourself,** don't carry this alone. Help your friend choose an adult to tell, and offer to go with them

[What the law does for under-18s](/law/under-18/)

## If they are 18 or older

What happens next is their decision. Medical care, a police report and a trial are separate choices. Help them see the options and respect what they choose, even if you would choose differently.

[What the law says for adults](/law/adults/)

## Look after yourself too

Hearing this is hard. It is fine to talk to a counsellor about how you are feeling. Tele-MANAS gives free mental health support at any hour on {% include call.html line="mental-health" %}.
