Four rights that most people are never told about. None of them depends on how much money you have.

## Free medical treatment

- **Every hospital, government or private, must treat a survivor of rape or child sexual abuse straight away and free of cost.** Refusing is a crime
- **You do not need a police report first**
- The hospital has to inform the police. If you are 18 or older, you still choose whether to make a complaint
- **Nothing is done without your consent.** You can agree to some parts of an examination and refuse others, and you can stop at any point
- You can ask for a woman doctor. A girl under 18 must be examined by a woman doctor
- **The "two-finger test" is banned.** No doctor is allowed to do it
- For the examination used as evidence, go to a government hospital. The law says a government doctor should do it where one is available, and that is what the police and courts rely on

## A free lawyer

- **Every woman and every child is entitled to a free lawyer, whatever their income.** There is nothing to prove and nothing to pay
- Others can also qualify, including people with disabilities, members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, and people on low incomes
- Ask at the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) at your district court, or call the legal aid helpline on {% include call.html line="legal-aid" %}
- Legal aid can also help if the police refuse to register your complaint

## Compensation

- **You may be able to get money from the government.** It is separate from any punishment of the person who did this, and it does not depend on a conviction
- Part of it can be paid early, before the case ends
- You apply through the Legal Services Authority. They can help you with the form
- **Apply as early as you can.** In the national scheme for women survivors, the time limit is three years from the crime or from the end of the trial. A late application can sometimes still be accepted, but don't count on it
- The amount depends on what happened and on which state or territory's scheme applies

## Your name stays private

- **After rape, or any sexual offence against someone under 18, nobody may publish your name, photo, address, school or anything else that could identify you.** This applies to newspapers, TV and social media. Doing it is a crime, and it can be reported
- Rape trials and cases involving children are heard in a **closed court**. The public and the press are not let in
- A child will not have to see the accused in court. An adult can ask the court for arrangements, such as a screen or a video link, so that she does not have to face the accused

## Who these rights apply to

The rights on this page apply in full to women and to everyone under 18. If you are a man or a transgender adult, some of them work differently. Call {% include call.html line="legal-aid" %} and ask what applies to you.
