**Medical care, a police report and a trial are separate decisions, and each one is yours.** You can get treated without reporting. You can report later. Nobody can make these choices for you.
{: .key}

## Who this page covers

The rape and sexual harassment offences in the BNS protect **women**. If you are a man or a transgender person, the law gives you less, and that is a real gap. Other laws can still help: the laws on assault and threats, the IT Act for images shared online, and the Transgender Persons Act. Call the legal aid helpline on {% include call.html line="legal-aid" %} and ask what applies to you.

If you are under 18, the law is different and it protects everyone equally. [Read the under-18 page](/law/under-18/).

## The main offences

| Unwanted sexual touching, or force used with sexual intent | Assault to outrage modesty | 74 |
| Unwelcome touching or advances, asking for sexual favours, showing you pornography, sexual remarks | Sexual harassment | 75 |

| Words, sounds or gestures meant to insult you sexually, or intruding on your privacy | Insulting modesty | 79 |

**Consent** means clearly and freely agreeing. The law says that not fighting back is not consent. Consent cannot be given by someone who is asleep, unconscious, too drunk to understand, or threatened.

## If it is your husband

**There is a gap in the law here.** Indian law still says that if a husband forces sex or sexual acts on his wife, it is not rape, as long as she is 18 or older. Cases challenging this rule are in the Supreme Court, which has not decided yet.

You still have protection:

- **If you are under 18, it is rape,** even though he is your husband
- **If you are living apart from him,** whether or not a court has ordered it, sex without your consent is a crime. It is punished with two to seven years in prison
- **Sexual abuse by a husband is domestic violence.** Under the Domestic Violence Act, a Magistrate can order him to stop, let you keep living in your home, and make him pay you money to live on. A Protection Officer, the One Stop Centre or a free lawyer can help you apply
- **Cruelty by a husband is a crime,** and that includes treating you in a way that could seriously harm your body or your mental health

Call the women's helpline on {% include call.html line="women" %} or the legal aid helpline on {% include call.html line="legal-aid" %}.

## Reporting to the police: FIR and Zero FIR

An FIR (First Information Report) is the written police record that starts an investigation.

- **You can file it at any police station, wherever it happened.** This is called a Zero FIR. The police pass it to the right station afterwards
- **You get a free copy**
- **There is no time limit for reporting rape.** Some less serious offences do have time limits, so speak to a lawyer early
- **If the police refuse to register it,** you can write to the Superintendent of Police. If that does not work, you can go to a Magistrate. Free legal aid can help you do both. For rape and most of the offences above, an officer who refuses to record your complaint is committing an offence

## Your right to a woman officer

- Your complaint about any of these offences must be written down by a **woman police officer**
- If you are reporting rape, your statement is recorded **at your home or a place you choose**, by a woman officer as far as possible. A parent, relative or social worker can be with you
- The police cannot make a woman come to a police station to be questioned. They must come to where she lives, unless she chooses to go to the station
- A Magistrate will also record your statement, by a woman Magistrate where possible
